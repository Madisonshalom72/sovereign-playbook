How to Do a UCC Search in California

By Madison Hoskins • UCC Talk24 • Updated October 13, 2026 • Educational guide — not legal advice

A UCC search tells you what financing statements are on record against a debtor in California — who claims an interest in their equipment, inventory, and receivables, and in what order. Here's how to run one correctly in CA: the official portal, the exact steps, the fees, and the one detail (the debtor's exact name) that decides whether your search result means anything.

How to run a California UCC search, step by step

  1. Open the bizfile Online UCC search above.
  2. Search by debtor name, secured party name, or file number. Refine with the advanced filters (status, file type, file date, lapse date) when the name is common.
  3. Start with the free non-standard search to catch near-matches and name variants — then understand that the legally significant test is the standard search logic result on the debtor's exact legal name.
  4. Open each record for file number, file/lapse dates, parties, status, and document images ($1 per document to view/print).
  5. For an official result, order a debtor search certificate (Form UCC11): $5 online, $10 paper.

California quirk: two kinds of “search,” two different answers

California offers both a non-standard (wild-card) search — free, broad, unofficial — and a standard search logic inquiry, which is the one the §9-506 safe harbor is measured against. A filing can appear in the wild-card search and still be seriously misleading if the standard search under the correct name wouldn't disclose it. Searchers: browse wide, then verify with the standard search.

What a CA UCC search does — and doesn't — prove

A search shows the financing statements a filing office has on record for the name you searched, as of the search's through-date. It is only as good as the name: under §9-506, a filing with a seriously misleading debtor name is ineffective, and a standard search under the debtor's correct name that wouldn't disclose it is the legal test. So always search the exact legal name (§9-503) — and when a result matters (a loan, a purchase, a credit decision), get the certified/official search rather than relying on a quick online look. A search also won't show filings in other states: a debtor is searched where the debtor is legally located under §9-307 — see the UCC search hub for other states.

California UCC search FAQ

How do I do a UCC search in California?

On the Secretary of State's bizfile Online portal (UCC search): search free by debtor name with the non-standard search, refine with advanced filters, and view document images for $1. Order a Form UCC11 debtor search certificate ($5 online) for an official result.

How much does a California UCC search cost?

The online non-standard search is free. A standard-logic debtor inquiry is $5 (waived when a certificate is ordered with it); certificates are $5 online or $10 on paper.

Where are UCC-1s filed in California?

Centrally with the California Secretary of State in Sacramento, online through bizfile Online (UCC Connect) or by mail.
