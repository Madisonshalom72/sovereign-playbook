UCC Continuation Statement: How to File a UCC-3 Before Your Filing Lapses

By Madison Hoskins • UCC Talk24 • Updated October 13, 2026 • Educational guide — not legal advice

Every UCC-1 financing statement has an expiration date: 5 years from the day it was filed. The only way to keep it alive is a continuation statement on the UCC-3 form — and the Code gives you a strict, unforgiving window to file it. File too early and it doesn't count. File one day late and your filing is dead, along with the priority position you held for five years. Here's exactly how continuation works and how to never miss the window.

The continuation rule (§9-515)

Under §9-515(a), a filed financing statement is effective for 5 years after the date of filing. (Two narrow exceptions run 30 years: filings for a public-finance transaction or a manufactured-home transaction, when the form says so.)

§9-515(d) sets the rule that trips people up: a continuation statement may be filed only within 6 months before the lapse date. Not a year before. Not "any time during year five." Six months, measured back from the exact 5-year anniversary of your filing date.

File a timely continuation and §9-515(e) extends the statement's effectiveness for 5 more years, starting on the original lapse date — not on the day you filed the continuation. File in month 55 of 60 and you still get the full extension to year 10. Continuations can be chained every 5 years, indefinitely, as long as each one lands inside its window.

One more requirement: only the secured party of record may file a continuation. If the loan was assigned, make sure the assignment was recorded (also on a UCC-3) so the correct party is "of record" before the window opens.

Worked example: computing your window

EventDate
UCC-1 filedMarch 14, 2023
Lapse date (5 years)March 14, 2028
Continuation window opens (6 months before)September 14, 2027
Continuation filed (example)November 2, 2027
New lapse date (5 years from original lapse date)March 14, 2033
Next window opensSeptember 14, 2032

Notice what the example proves: filing the continuation "early" inside the window costs you nothing — the extension always runs from the original lapse date. There is no benefit to waiting until the last week, and a mountain of risk. File in the first month of the window.

The two ways people blow it

Too early: a continuation filed before the 6-month window opens is ineffective — the filing office may accept and stamp it, and it still doesn't extend anything. This happens when staff calendar "5 years" from the wrong date or file a continuation alongside an unrelated amendment in year 3.

Too late: there is no grace period, no cure provision, and no reinstatement. A continuation filed after the lapse date cannot revive the original filing. Ever.

How to file a UCC-3 continuation in 5 steps

  1. Pull the original filing record. Run a UCC search on the debtor's exact legal name in the filing state and confirm: the initial file number, the exact file date/time, the current lapse date, and that you (or your assignor chain) are the secured party of record. Do not rely on your memory of the file number — pull it from the record.
  2. Confirm you're inside the window. Today must be on or after the date 6 months before the lapse date, and before the lapse date itself. If the window hasn't opened, calendar it — don't file yet.
  3. Complete the UCC-3. Enter the initial financing statement's file number in Item 1 exactly as it appears on the record, check the Continuation box (Item 3 on the national form), and complete the secured-party-of-record authorization block (Item 9). A continuation does not change parties or collateral — it only extends. If you also need changes, that's a separate amendment action on the same form.
  4. File and pay. Submit through the same central filing office (Secretary of State) where the UCC-1 lives — most states accept UCC-3s through their online portal, usually for a modest fee comparable to an amendment. File in the wrong state and the continuation attaches to nothing.
  5. Verify and re-calendar. After filing, search the record again and confirm the lapse date now shows the new date 5 years out. Then immediately calendar the next window: new lapse date, and 6 months before it. Keep the stamped acknowledgment with the loan file.

What happens if your filing lapses

§9-515(c) is blunt: when a financing statement lapses, it becomes ineffective, and the security interest perfected by it becomes unperfected. Worse, the interest "is deemed never to have been perfected" against a purchaser of the collateral who gave value — meaning a later lender or buyer can jump ahead of you as if your five years of perfection never existed.

Your options after a lapse are damage control, not repair:

  • File a new UCC-1 immediately. It perfects from the new filing date only. Your priority date resets to today, behind everyone who filed in the gap.
  • Check what moved in the gap. Search the debtor's record for new filings, and check whether the debtor granted interests or sold collateral while you were unperfected.
  • Review the security agreement. The underlying agreement usually survives — you still have a contract claim. What you lost is perfection and priority, not the debt itself.

A deadline system that won't fail you

Continuation failures are never knowledge failures — they're calendaring failures. A minimal system that works:

  • At filing: record three dates for every UCC-1 — the lapse date, the window-open date (lapse minus 6 months), and a "verify" reminder 30 days before the lapse date.
  • Two independent reminders for the window-open date (calendar + a tickler in your loan file). If one system dies, the other catches it.
  • Quarterly portfolio sweep: once a quarter, list every filing that lapses in the next 12 months and confirm who owns the continuation task.
  • On any assignment or servicing transfer: the lapse dates move with the file — put them in the transfer checklist, in writing.

Continuation FAQ

When can I file a UCC continuation statement?

Only within the 6 months before the 5-year lapse date (§9-515(d)). Earlier is ineffective; later is impossible.

What happens if my UCC-1 lapses?

The financing statement becomes ineffective and the security interest becomes unperfected — treated as never perfected against a purchaser who gave value (§9-515(c)). A new UCC-1 gets a new, junior priority date.

How long does a continuation extend a UCC filing?

Five years from the original lapse date (§9-515(e)), no matter when inside the window you file. Continuations can repeat every 5 years indefinitely.

Who can file a UCC continuation statement?

The secured party of record, on the UCC-3 form or the state's online portal, identifying the initial financing statement by file number. The debtor does not need to sign anything new.
