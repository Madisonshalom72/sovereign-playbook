How to Tokenize a Promissory Note

By Madison Hoskins • UCC Talk24 • Published November 16, 2026 • Educational guide — not legal advice

Learning how to tokenize a promissory note starts with the sentence most token pitches skip: the token is the record; the agreement is the right. Tokenization does not create a payment right, erase a debt, or mint money — it gives an existing promissory note a new carrier: a controllable electronic record under UCC Article 12, so the right to be paid can move at the speed of a ledger instead of the speed of the mail. Done in the right order, the process is eight steps — and the order is the discipline.

Two roads for one note: Article 3 paper, Article 12 record

A promissory note is the oldest instrument in commerce: a maker's signed, written promise to pay. When it is an unconditional promise to pay a fixed amount of money, payable to order or to bearer, it is a negotiable instrument under §3-104 — Article 3 paper, transferred by delivery and indorsement, with the holder-in-due-course doctrine standing behind a good-faith buyer. That road still runs. Its purely electronic twin, though, does not run through Article 12: an electronic note travels under the "transferable record" provisions of the federal E-SIGN Act and state UETA, which carry their own control test — and that is exactly why "transferable record" is excluded from the controllable electronic record definition in §12-102.

The Article 12 road is different engineering. Keep the note as the agreement, but evidence its payment right with a controllable electronic record — defined in §12-102(a)(1) as "a record stored in an electronic medium that can be subjected to control under Section 12-105." The drafting move that makes it work is one sentence in the paper: the maker undertakes to pay the person who has control of the record. That undertaking makes the payment right a controllable payment intangible (§9-102(a)(27B)) — or, for an invoice, a controllable account (§9-102(a)(27A)).

And here is the payoff, §12-104 working as designed: a qualifying purchaser — one who obtains control of the record for value, in good faith, and without notice of a property claim (§12-102(a)(2)) — takes the payment right itself free of property claims (§12-104(e)). That is the holder-in-due-course result, delivered through the token. The Code even protects the buyer's good faith against the filing office: under §12-104(h), a financing statement on file is not notice of a claim. And §12-106 tells the maker where the money goes — to the person having control, and after a proper signed notification of transfer, to the transferee; paying the old holder after notice does not discharge the debt.

The one-sentence version

The token is the record; the agreement is the right. Tokenization moves the record. The right moves because the agreement and the Code move it — never because a token says so.

The tokenization process, step by step

Everything above was the map; here is the road, in the only order it works. Each step states what actually happens, the paper or record it produces, and the Code hook where one exists. Skip a step, or run one out of order, and you have not found a shortcut — you have found the place the deal will break.

Step 1 — Choose the asset, and prove the rights in it

What happens: one specific note is selected, and the person who would tokenize it proves he holds it — the original instrument, every assignment down the chain to him, and a lien search showing no earlier pledge hiding in the file.

The paper it produces: the title file — originals, assignments, search results — the evidence everything else stands on.

The Code hook: §9-203 makes rights in the collateral an element of attachment, and the same logic runs through the whole process. You cannot tokenize what you cannot prove.

Step 2 — Structure the legal wrapper

What happens: the parties decide who will hold the note while it lives on the rail — the owner himself, a trust, or a special-purpose company formed to hold this one asset and nothing else — and the note is formally placed in that holder's hands.

The paper it produces: the wrapper's own documents (the company charter and operating agreement, or the trust instrument) and the written contribution or assignment that moves the note into it.

The Code hook: the wrapper becomes the named holder for everything downstream — the debtor a financing statement must name correctly under §9-503, and the person whose control arrangements §12-105 will measure. A filing against the wrong name is a filing against nobody.

Step 3 — Value it

What happens: the note is priced by evidence, not by hope — a discounting of the payment stream, with the risks written down next to the number: the maker's credit, the time to maturity, the collateral behind it.

The paper it produces: the valuation record, kept in the deal file where a later buyer, lender, or court can find it.

The Code hook: value is a working element of the system, not a courtesy — attachment requires value given (§9-203), and a qualifying purchaser is defined as one who obtains control for value (§12-102(a)(2)). The man who documented his value can prove his seat; the man who cannot, argues.

Step 4 — Write the terms

What happens: the operative agreement is drafted and signed, stating exactly what a token holder owns or is owed — the amount, the payment dates, the redemption right, what happens on default — before any token exists.

The paper it produces: the instrument itself — the note, or the token terms agreement that every later record points back to.

The Code hook: this is where the road is chosen and named plainly. A negotiable note runs on Article 3, its electronic twin on the transferable-record rules of E-SIGN and UETA. A payment right evidenced by a record runs on Article 12, and the maker's undertaking to pay the person in control is what makes it a controllable payment intangible (§9-102(a)(27B)). And if what is being sold is an interest in a business's profits, Howey is standing in this step: it is a security, and securities law governs its sale.

Step 5 — Create the token

What happens: only now is the smart contract deployed — total supply set, transfer rules coded, and an associated record created that identifies the note and points to the Step 4 terms, ideally by the terms' own hash, so any later change to the document shows.

The record it produces: the controllable electronic record itself, and its associated record — the pointer from the token to the paper.

The Code hook: §12-102(a)(1) — the record is a CER only if it can be subjected to control under §12-105, which is why the contract's transfer mechanics are a legal fact, not a design preference. Say it once more, at the exact step where men forget it: the token is the record; the agreement from Step 4 is the right.

Step 6 — Perfect and protect

What happens: the interest the world must respect is perfected, and control of the record is locked down — a UCC-1 financing statement filed where filing applies, control of the CER taken and kept, and the keys placed under a written custody arrangement: held by the owner alone, split by multisignature, or held by a custodian who acknowledges in writing that he holds control on the holder's behalf.

The paper it produces: the filed financing statement, and the custodian's written acknowledgment.

The Code hook: perfection by filing (§9-312) and by control (§9-314, measured by §12-105) — and §9-326A: control beats filing when the two collide. Who holds control is who holds power; this step is where power is assigned, in writing.

Step 7 — Issue and transfer

What happens: the token is issued against payment to its first holder, and every later transfer moves control on the ledger to the next holder — with the maker told, in the statutory form, that the payment right has moved and where to pay now.

The record it produces: the ledger's transfer history, and the signed notification of transfer served on the maker.

The Code hook: the buyer who takes control for value, in good faith, without notice of a property claim is a qualifying purchaser and takes free of that claim (§12-104(e)) — but only because Steps 1 through 6 were done in order. And §12-106 protects the payment itself: the maker who pays the person in control, before proper notification, has discharged the debt.

Step 8 — Service and redeem

What happens: the deal is lived out — installments collected by the person in control, statements kept, defaults noticed and worked — until payoff or maturity, when the obligation is satisfied and the structure is taken down as deliberately as it was built.

The paper it produces: the payment records, the satisfaction or release, and the termination filing.

The Code hook: payment to the person in control discharges the maker (§12-106), and when the debt is gone the token is burned or retired, so the record can never circulate as if the right still lived — while a UCC-3 termination statement (§9-513) clears the financing statement from the public record. A process that began with proof ends with proof: nothing left trading, and nothing left filed, that says a dead right is alive.

Why tokenize a note at all — and where it stops

The honest case for the work is threefold. Settlement in minutes instead of weeks of couriering originals. One true record of the note's state that every party can check — what Project Promissa, the Bank for International Settlements' proof of concept with the World Bank and the Swiss National Bank for tokenizing the promissory notes member countries give multilateral banks, calls a "golden record." The paper world solved the identical problem with possession of the single original: there could be only one holder because there was only one piece of paper. Control is that singularity, rebuilt — which is why §12-105 demands the exclusive powers. And fractional interests a paper note cannot practically carry. In Mexico, the firm Mifiel reports issuing tokenized promissory notes in production since March 2023 — tens of thousands of them — because the paper process there was slow and the double-pledge risk real: a unique token linked to each note makes pledging the same note twice visible.

Now the limits, with the same plainness. Under §12-104(f), outside the controllable account and payment intangible design, a qualifying purchaser takes the rights evidenced by a record subject to property claims in those underlying rights — the token is cleansed; the thing it points at is not automatically. Tokenize an interest in land and Article 12 governs the token — the land itself still moves by deed, at the recorder's office. Tokenize a bad note and you own a bad note with better marketing. The code performs; the courts still interpret; the maker's defenses did not evaporate because his promise learned to travel.

The hard limits — read this twice

Tokenizing does not erase securities law. Under SEC v. W.J. Howey Co., 328 U.S. 293 (1946), an investment of money in a common enterprise with profits expected from the efforts of others is a security, whatever it is called. Sell the public a token on promises of profit from your team's efforts and you are offering a security, which requires registration or an exemption.

No token is money you issue yourself. A private token is property and contract, not legal tender — under the amended Code itself, private crypto is not "money"; it is a controllable electronic record. No filing, seal, or declaration turns it into currency.

No token makes a debt disappear. Obligations end by payment, release, or law — never by minting a record that says they ended.

Nothing here is investment advice, and no token, coin, or platform named on this page is recommended or promoted.

Article 12 is state law or it is nothing. The 2022 UCC amendments have been enacted in 36 states + DC as of October 2026 (Uniform Law Commission enactment table, tallied October 1, 2026); Pennsylvania enacted it as Act 41 of 2024, effective August 30, 2024, where Article 12 sits as Division 12 of Title 13. In a state that has not enacted the amendments, Article 12 does not exist as law, and the older general-intangible rules still run. Never say "Article 12 provides" without naming the state whose statute you are reading.

Tokenized promissory note FAQ

What does it mean to tokenize a promissory note?

It means evidencing the note's payment right with a controllable electronic record under UCC Article 12, so the right can be transferred by transferring control of the record instead of couriering paper. The note — the signed agreement — still creates the right. The token is the record; the agreement is the right.

Is a tokenized promissory note still an Article 3 negotiable instrument?

The note itself can be: a negotiable instrument is a signed, unconditional promise to pay a fixed amount of money, payable to order or bearer (§3-104). Its purely electronic twin runs through the transferable-record provisions of the federal E-SIGN Act and state UETA — which is why "transferable record" is excluded from the CER definition in §12-102. The Article 12 design is different engineering: the note stays the agreement, and its payment right is evidenced by a record in which the maker undertakes to pay the person who has control — a controllable payment intangible under §9-102(a)(27B).

What is a controllable electronic record?

Under §12-102(a)(1), a controllable electronic record is "a record stored in an electronic medium that can be subjected to control under Section 12-105." Control under §12-105 means the record or its system gives one person the power to take substantially all the benefit of the record, the exclusive power to keep others from that benefit, the exclusive power to transfer control, and the ability to identify itself as the person holding those powers. It is possession, rebuilt for a record.

Does tokenizing a promissory note make it a security?

Not by itself — tokenizing your own note's payment right is a transfer-and-perfection design. But selling interests to investors who expect profits from the efforts of others is an investment contract — a security — under SEC v. W.J. Howey Co., 328 U.S. 293 (1946), whatever the token is called. Securities law stands over the whole field and requires registration or an exemption.

Which states have UCC Article 12?

The 2022 UCC amendments, including Article 12, have been enacted in 36 states and the District of Columbia as of October 2026 (Uniform Law Commission enactment table, tallied October 1, 2026). Pennsylvania enacted them as Act 41 of 2024, effective August 30, 2024, where Article 12 sits as Division 12 of Title 13. In a state that has not enacted the amendments, Article 12 does not exist as law and the older general-intangible rules still run.

Bottom line

How to tokenize a promissory note is not a minting trick — it is the oldest discipline in this field wearing the newest coat: prove the rights, paper the agreement, perfect by filing and by control — because control beats filing — and redeem as cleanly as you issued. Paper first, token second, perfection always.
