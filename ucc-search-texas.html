How to Do a UCC Search in Texas

By Madison Hoskins • UCC Talk24 • Updated October 13, 2026 • Educational guide — not legal advice

A UCC search tells you what financing statements are on record against a debtor in Texas — who claims an interest in their equipment, inventory, and receivables, and in what order. Here's how to run one correctly in TX: the official portal, the exact steps, the fees, and the one detail (the debtor's exact name) that decides whether your search result means anything.

Filing officeTexas Secretary of State — UCC Section (Austin)
Official portalSOSDirect — Texas Secretary of State online business services
Account needed?Yes — SOSDirect requires an account (a temporary login option exists for one-off use).
Search feesSOSDirect charges a $1 fee per search (statutory system fee). Texas is also rolling out SOSPortal as SOSDirect's replacement — either route starts at sos.state.tx.us.
Where UCC-1s are filedCentral filing with the Secretary of State. UCC filings are submitted through SOSDirect/SOSPortal or by mail to the UCC Section in Austin.

How to run a Texas UCC search, step by step

  1. Open SOSDirect above and log in (or request an account / use the temporary login for a one-off search). Note Texas is migrating users to the new SOSPortal — follow the Secretary of State site's current link.
  2. Go to the UCC search function and search by the debtor's exact legal name — or by file number if you have it.
  3. Review each financing statement: file number, file and lapse dates, debtor and secured party exactly as indexed, collateral, and all UCC-3 activity.
  4. Order copies or a search certificate through the system if the transaction requires an official record.
  5. Mind the fee mechanics: SOSDirect charges per search, so plan your name variants in advance instead of paying to fish.

Texas quirk: file in Texas, but think about where the debtor “is”

Texas debtors confuse out-of-state lenders constantly: a Texas LLC that operates in three states is still “located” in Texas for UCC purposes — its state of organization controls under §9-307. File where the debtor is legally located, not where the equipment sits — except fixture filings, which go to the county clerk where the land is.

What a TX UCC search does — and doesn't — prove

A search shows the financing statements a filing office has on record for the name you searched, as of the search's through-date. It is only as good as the name: under §9-506, a filing with a seriously misleading debtor name is ineffective, and a standard search under the debtor's correct name that wouldn't disclose it is the legal test. So always search the exact legal name (§9-503) — and when a result matters (a loan, a purchase, a credit decision), get the certified/official search rather than relying on a quick online look. A search also won't show filings in other states: a debtor is searched where the debtor is legally located under §9-307 — see the UCC search hub for other states.

Texas UCC search FAQ

How do I do a UCC search in Texas?

Through the Texas Secretary of State's online system (SOSDirect, now migrating to SOSPortal). Log in, use the UCC search, and search by the debtor's exact legal name or file number; searches carry a $1 per-search fee.

Where are UCC-1s filed in Texas?

Centrally with the Texas Secretary of State (UCC Section, Austin) — online through SOSDirect/SOSPortal or by mail. Fixture filings are recorded with the county clerk.

How much does a Texas UCC search cost?

$1 per search through SOSDirect, plus fees for copies or certificates if you order them.
