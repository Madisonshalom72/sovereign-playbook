How to Buy Luzerne County Repository Sale Properties for $500–$1,000

The third stage of the county tax sale — where the parcels nobody bid on wait for the buyer who did his homework.

Every year, luzerne county repository sale properties sit in a county inventory that most buyers never learn exists — roughly 1,000 parcels that went to sale twice, drew no buyer, and now wait with the Tax Claim Bureau. One county repository list counted 1,167 parcels, with a median assessed value of $13,900. Minimum bids start at $500–$1,000. This page teaches the ladder, the buying steps, and — just as important — the traps.

The InventoryWhat the Repository Is

When a Luzerne County property owner falls behind on taxes, the parcel moves through a system run by the Luzerne County Tax Claim Bureau. It is offered at public sale. If it doesn't sell, it's offered again, harder. If it still doesn't sell, it lands in the repository — the county's holding inventory, where parcels can be bought by application at set minimum prices instead of by auction bidding. Serious local investors study this list. Out-of-state course sellers have started teaching this county by name. Almost nobody explains it in plain English — until now.

The SystemThe Ladder: Upset → Judicial → Repository

Every repository parcel climbed down the same three rungs. Which rung a property is on decides what happens to its liens — and what you actually pay.

StageWhat it isLiensPrice
1 · Upset Sale The first public sale. The opening price is the upset price — the taxes and claims owed. Sold subject to existing liens and mortgages — they do not wash off. Starts at the full upset price; competitive bidding can push it up.
2 · Judicial Sale Parcels that didn't sell at upset go before the court for a second sale. Sold free and clear of most liens — the court order is what clears them. Starts lower; the county wants these moved.
3 · Repository Still unsold. The parcel now sits in the Tax Claim Bureau's repository inventory. Carries the judicial-sale posture — but title research is still on you (see the traps). Set minimum bids of $500–$1,000. No auction. First qualified bidder.

The ProcessHow Buying Works

The steps below are per the county's Repository Sales Policy, as published by the Tax Claim Bureau. Policies change — confirm the current terms with the Tax Claim Bureau before you bid.

Pick from the current list

Work only from the Bureau's current repository list — parcel number (PIN), municipality, and assessed value. Old lists are teaching specimens, not inventory.

Bid the minimum

Minimum bids run $500 to $1,000 depending on the parcel, plus a $100 application fee. The rule is first-bidder: the first qualified application at the set price takes the parcel — there is no bidding war.

Survive the hold

Your bid sits through a 5-day hold. And if a parcel sits unsold, the policy provides a price drop after 3 months — patience is a strategy here.

Clear three approvals

A repository sale needs sign-off from three districts: the municipality, the school district, and the county. Each one reviews the bid on its own calendar.

Wait out the timeline

From application to deed, plan on 9 to 12 months. This is slow money by design — the men who win here are the men who can wait.

Read This TwiceThe Traps

Many parcels are slivers

A large share of the repository is unbuildable land — paper streets, hillside strips, landlocked leftovers. A $500 parcel you can never build on, sell, or use is not a deal at any price.

You cannot inspect the inside

There are no showings. You buy the parcel as it sits — condition, occupants, and surprises included. Price that blindness into every bid.

Title research is on you

The county sells its interest; verifying what you are actually getting — deed history, parcel boundaries, what survived — is the buyer's job, at the buyer's cost.

Assessed value is not market value

A $13,900 assessed value is a tax number, not a selling price. And the entry price is only the first cost: taxes, fees, cleanup, and title work stack on top of it before the parcel owes you anything back.

No page — including this one — can promise what a parcel will return. The repository rewards research and patience, and it punishes guessing. Learn the system first; bid second.

QuestionsAsked and Answered

What is a repository property?

A parcel that was offered at Luzerne County's upset sale and then at judicial sale, did not sell at either, and now sits in the repository held by the Luzerne County Tax Claim Bureau — where it can be bought by application instead of at auction.

How much do repository properties cost?

Per the county's repository policy, minimum bids run $500 to $1,000 depending on the parcel, plus a $100 application fee. The purchase price is only the first cost — taxes, fees, cleanup, and title work stack on top. Confirm current terms with the Tax Claim Bureau before bidding.

How long does approval take?

Plan on 9 to 12 months. A repository bid needs approvals from three districts — the municipality, the school district, and the county — and the policy includes a 5-day hold and a price drop after 3 months.

Can anyone buy?

Generally yes — the repository is open to the public, and the county's policy works on a first-bidder rule. Read the current Repository Sales Policy and confirm eligibility and terms with the Tax Claim Bureau before you apply.

Where is the current list?

On the Luzerne County Tax Claim Bureau's official website: luzernecountytaxclaim.com. The list changes as parcels are sold and added — always work from the Bureau's current list, not a copied or dated one.
