How to Do a UCC Search in New York

By Madison Hoskins • UCC Talk24 • Updated October 13, 2026 • Educational guide — not legal advice

A UCC search tells you what financing statements are on record against a debtor in New York — who claims an interest in their equipment, inventory, and receivables, and in what order. Here's how to run one correctly in NY: the official portal, the exact steps, the fees, and the one detail (the debtor's exact name) that decides whether your search result means anything.

Filing officeNew York Department of State — Division of Corporations, State Records and Uniform Commercial Code (Albany)
Official portalNY DOS UCC Public Search (online debtor search)
Account needed?No account for the free public online search. Official searches are requested on Form UCC-11, one form and fee per name.
Search feesOfficial search: $25 per debtor name (Form UCC-11); $50 for search results issued under seal. Copies: $5 plain / $10 certified. The free online public search above is unofficial.
Where UCC-1s are filedCentral filing with the Department of State in Albany. Paper UCC filing: $40; electronic filing (e-File/XML): $20 (per the DOS fee schedule).

How to run a New York UCC search, step by step

  1. Start with the free online public search above to see what's on record: search by debtor name, or by filing number and date.
  2. Search the debtor's exact legal name. New York's standard search logic returns exact-name matches — a near-miss spelling returns nothing, which is precisely why exact names matter on filings.
  3. Record each hit's file number, file date, lapse date, secured party, and any UCC-3 activity (amendments, continuations, terminations).
  4. Treat the online result as a preview. For a transaction that depends on it, order the official search: submit Form UCC-11 to the Department of State with the $25 per-name fee (one form per name).
  5. If you need the documents themselves, order copies ($5 plain, $10 certified per document).

New York quirk: exact-name search logic cuts both ways

The Department of State states it plainly: its standard search logic returns the exact name requested. For searchers, that means one wrong letter = a falsely “clean” result. For filers, it means a debtor-name error that a standard search can't find makes the filing seriously misleading under §9-506 — New York is the last place to get casual about spelling.

What a NY UCC search does — and doesn't — prove

A search shows the financing statements a filing office has on record for the name you searched, as of the search's through-date. It is only as good as the name: under §9-506, a filing with a seriously misleading debtor name is ineffective, and a standard search under the debtor's correct name that wouldn't disclose it is the legal test. So always search the exact legal name (§9-503) — and when a result matters (a loan, a purchase, a credit decision), get the certified/official search rather than relying on a quick online look. A search also won't show filings in other states: a debtor is searched where the debtor is legally located under §9-307 — see the UCC search hub for other states.

New York UCC search FAQ

How do I do a UCC search in New York?

Use the Department of State's free online UCC public search for a quick look, searching the debtor's exact legal name. For an official result, submit Form UCC-11 (Information Request) to the DOS UCC unit — $25 per debtor name, one form per name.

How much does a New York UCC search cost?

$25 per debtor name for an official search ($50 under seal). The online public search is free but unofficial. Copies are $5 plain and $10 certified.

Where are UCC-1s filed in New York?

Centrally with the Department of State in Albany: $40 for a paper filing, $20 for an electronic filing, per the DOS UCC fee schedule.
