How to build a million dollar trust fund is one of the most-asked wealth questions online — and one of the least answered. The results that rank for it are written for couples who already have millions in excess wealth, while the comment sections underneath fill up with ordinary people asking where a trust is established, who qualifies, and what order the pieces go in. A new book written for those commenters, “Generate a $1,000,000 in 30 Days or Less” by Madison Hoskins, takes the question head-on — and says the order most people assume is backwards.

Prepare, protect, maintain — in that order

The book is not written to a reader chasing a million. It is written to a reader who has one — on paper, the day his position is structured — and whose real job is the three disciplines in its tagline: Start. Manage. Grow. Its governing line is printed early and repeated often: “Money is attracted to good management and runs away from bad management.”

The structure the book teaches at headline level: the trust is created before the policy, the policy becomes the reader’s own bank, and the capital is deployed into undervalued property — with the management systems to keep what gets built. The steps, the figures, and the plays themselves are the book: 332 pages, 40 chapters and two appendices, including an AI prompt pack and a verified resource list of 16 life insurance carriers.

The second question nobody answers

The release’s research also surfaced a second unanswered question: can you use a promissory note as collateral for a loan? Search results answer the opposite question — how a lender takes collateral for a note — while the holder’s side of the table, pledging a note you hold, goes untaught in public. The book gives that question its own chapter, alongside the alternate method it is known for among early readers. What the method is, and how the numbers run, is inside the book.

“People keep asking how to build a million dollar trust fund like the money comes first and the structure comes later. It’s the other way around. Prepare the structure, protect what it holds, then maintain it like a man who intends to keep it — that’s the whole game, and it’s why the book is priced where a working man can reach it.”— Madison Hoskins, author

Frequently asked questions

Q: How do you build a million dollar trust fund?
A: That is the book’s title question, and its answer starts with sequence: what gets created first, what it holds, and how it is managed afterward. The full structure — chapters, figures, and plays — is the book itself, available at the link above.
Q: Can you use a promissory note as collateral for a loan?
A: The book devotes a chapter to the holder’s side of that question — the side public results do not teach. The chapter is in the book; the question is why readers keep asking it in comment sections with no public answer.
Q: Should you create a trust before buying a life insurance policy?
A: The book’s position is in its framing: preparation comes before protection, and protection before growth. Which order, and why it matters, is laid out in the trust chapters.
Q: What is a million dollar life insurance policy, and is the money spendable on day one?
A: The book answers that question honestly in its opening chapters — what is real the day the policy binds, and what builds over time — because the honest version of that answer is the foundation everything else stands on.
Q: Is this infinite banking?
A: The book works in the same vehicle family but is not an infinite banking book: it adds the trust-first sequence, note collateral, and property deployment plays. The comparison is answered in full on the book’s page.
