The Case on the Record

Most drivers facing a citation never learn how to fight a traffic case without a lawyer, because nearly everything written on the subject is published by law firms selling representation. Hoskins wrote the opposite book. Standing pro se — no attorney — he filed his own objection to jurisdiction and controlled his own record, and his case, Commonwealth v. Madison Edward Hoskins (Docket No. CP-40-CR-0001237-2026), closed on September 21, 2026 with most of the charges dismissed: four of the five counts withdrawn, including the first-degree misdemeanor driving-while-suspended charge and the no-insurance charge. The one summary count that remained was answered with a no-contest plea, which admits no guilt.

Inside the Book

The bonus chapter, "The Secret to Jurisdiction," lays out the method in thirteen points: the question asked at the stop, "under threat and duress" written on the fingerprint card's signature line, the objection filed the moment the paperwork arrived, and the Two Questions for any case — Who is the injured party? What is the contract? It also takes up the question drivers ask and almost nobody answers directly — is a traffic ticket a contract — printing Black's Law Dictionary's own definitions of BOND and RECOGNIZANCE, and the Hebrew word underneath "bond," 'ĕsâr, an obligation that binds the soul.

A full Foundations section prints the First, Fourth, and Sixth Amendments in their own words with "the facts" for each, the injured-party doctrine, the six elements of a contract and the types of contract as they relate to court, Black's definition of WARRANT, and every type of jurisdiction — including jurisdiction by consent. An expanded glossary breaks down thirteen courtroom words from Black's — attorney, sui juris, magistrate, judge, bench, order, sovereign, sovereignty, en banc, name, likeness, trademark, and authorized representative — with each word's Hebrew, Latin, and Greek roots underneath it.

In "The Secrets to Your Name," the book teaches that your name is your property — along with your likeness, your fingerprints, and your DNA — anchored in the right of publicity and Pennsylvania's own statute, 42 Pa.C.S. § 8316, and explains the name as the trademark of an unfranchised business and the capacity a man should act in: authorized representative and beneficiary of his name, never its surety. A closing section, "The Secret to Going Private," draws the parallel to a public company delisting from the exchange.
