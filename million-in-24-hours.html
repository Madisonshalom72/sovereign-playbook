Can you use a promissory note as collateral for a loan?

Yes — a note you HOLD is an asset, and an asset can be pledged. The lender reads it five ways: the face amount and payment history, the strength of the maker, the collateral standing behind the note, the discount they will demand, and whether the assignment and filing are done right. Expect to borrow a fraction of the face, not the face — and Chapter 33 also teaches the alternate method whole: the million-dollar note, written and registered the right way. Can a life insurance policy be used as collateral?

Two different moves, and the difference matters. You can borrow FROM the policy itself — a policy loan against your own cash value, no committee, no credit inquiry. Or you can pledge the policy TO another lender by collateral assignment, so a bank lends against the position you hold. One borrows from your bank; the other borrows against it — and a million-dollar policy changes how you read to a lender before it is ever assigned. (Chapters 10–11) Both moves, worked line by line, are in the book — get it for $7.99 here.

The Gap Method buys paper — a note somebody owes on — at a discount, and the profit lives in the gap between what the paper costs and what it pays.
